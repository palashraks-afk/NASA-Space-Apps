## Decisions

1. **Real noise replaces assumed noise.** `lab/scripts/derive_noise.py` computes within-person SD and lag-1 autocorrelation per person. Medians: sleep duration SD 1.34 h, rho -0.02; sleep efficiency SD 0.035, rho 0.00; time to fall asleep SD 0.30 h, rho 0.02; daily median RMSSD SD 10.3 ms, rho 0.09 (32 people); daily median heart rate SD 6.1 bpm, rho 0.11 (32 people). Earlier simulations assumed rho 0.3, which was pessimistic for these outcomes.
2. **Wearable outcomes are daytime-weighted, not overnight.** The filtered data has few overnight segments, so an overnight measure is not supportable. Steps were dropped because filtered segments undercount them. A few 2018 timestamps (clock errors outside the 2021 study) were dropped.
3. **3D lab, first playable version.** Procedural station module (racks, lockers, cargo bags, vents, laptops, cables, LED corner strips, hatch, window), four stations, camera glides between them, HUD with a wrist checklist and a control deck, a plain 2D "numbers" panel, pre-registration export. Rendering: three.js with AgX tone mapping, ambient occlusion (GTAO), bloom limited to light sources, shadows; quality drops automatically if the frame rate stays under 32 fps.
4. **Problems found by looking at full-resolution renders, and fixed.** The cabin was washed out (bloom caught the white walls); the shell's end caps sealed the window; the evidence chamber looked empty because the glass wrote depth over the motes, then blew out to white because 1,600 additive motes stacked; tokens never finished sticking (the spring pulled to a point 3 cm off the board while the stick check measured to the board); token letters were rotated a quarter turn; the "B" in the marker font read as "8" at token size, so token faces use Archivo.
5. **Opening on a daylight pass.** The CelesTrak epoch falls on Earth's night side; the app starts the clock at the first time after the epoch when the station is sunlit with the Sun well above its horizon (same real orbit, later moment).
6. **Verdict read once, at the end of the mission.** The false-call rate was measured for a single read at the planned end, so the app shows probabilities every day but gives the verdict only on the last day. Peeking-and-stopping would raise the false-call rate; it is not offered.
7. **Testing while the browser pane is hidden.** Browsers pause animation frames in hidden tabs, so the app exposes a dev-only step function (`__solAdvance`) and a `?auto=<scenario>` driver; `lab/scripts/shoot.sh` takes full-resolution screenshots with headless Edge and a throwaway profile.
8. **Validation studies run, pre-registered first** (`docs/STUDIES_PREREG.md`, `docs/STUDIES_RESULTS.md`). Two bars failed and are reported as failures: plug-in crew pooling undercovered (fixed by integrating over between-person spread, Study 2b, pre-registered after the failure), and the circadian model locks to a Mars sol more easily than the pre-registered reading of the 2007 human study (the clock station now carries that caveat).
9. **Prior-art correction.** The Mars-sol light-timing result is published (2007); the app presents its own simulation as a replication, not a discovery.
10. **Crew mode added.** Four crew members, own randomized schedules, own verdicts, sharpened by validated pooling.
11. **A syntax error shipped once** (commit b219aca; a mismatched quote broke the whole app). Found because headless screenshots came back blank. `npm run check` now parses every file before commits.
12. **Simulated sleep data is now real people's data.** Gaussian noise produced impossible values (negative minutes to fall asleep). Each simulated person is now one of the 49 diary participants: their own typical level, with days resampled from their own logged deviations, kept within physical limits. Wearable measures keep the Gaussian model (values far from any limit).
13. **Redesigned as a professional crew health console** (feedback: "make everything look more like a professional tool", "hella buggy and glitchy"). Dark console layout: tabs along the top, an inspector on the right, a mission timeline at the bottom that drives every station (play, speed, crew selection). The floating HUD and wrist checklist were removed.
14. **Glitches traced and removed.** The clock deck was rebuilt every half second (clicks lost, open sections snapped shut); worker results re-rendered the whole deck (focus lost mid-typing); the camera drifted with the pointer, bobbed and rolled; floating objects crossed station views; a tab switch after the first one did nothing (a stale element reference). Panels now mount once and update values in place; the camera holds still between moves; laptops, floaters and cargo bags were cleared from in front of every station.
15. **Three new health stations, driven by one crew health model** (`lab/src/sim/health.js`, 5 tests). 14 pre-flight days plus 165 mission days for four crew. Medical: sleep from real diary participants; heart rate and HRV from published spreads; resting core temperature drifts up about 1 °C over 2.5 months (Stahn et al. 2017), shown as expected rather than flagged. Each measure is compared with that person's own pre-flight range (t prediction interval, clipped to physical limits). Air: CO2 with occasional reduced-scrubber days, the 3 mmHg nominal limit, and headache risk from the 7-day average (Law et al. 2014); temperature and humidity within NASA-STD-3001. Galley: a 14-item daily menu built from 19 USDA FoodData Central foods (pinned ids, local SR Legacy release), totals against NASA JSC-67378 targets. In-flight energy intake runs below targets, as reported for real crews.
16. **What is simulated is labelled where it is shown.** Every inspector panel ends with its sources and which values are simulated.
