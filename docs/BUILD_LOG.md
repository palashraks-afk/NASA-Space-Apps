## Decisions

1. **Real noise replaces assumed noise.** `lab/scripts/derive_noise.py` computes within-person SD and lag-1 autocorrelation per person. Medians: sleep duration SD 1.34 h, rho -0.02; sleep efficiency SD 0.035, rho 0.00; time to fall asleep SD 0.30 h, rho 0.02; daily median RMSSD SD 10.3 ms, rho 0.09 (32 people); daily median heart rate SD 6.1 bpm, rho 0.11 (32 people). Earlier simulations assumed rho 0.3, which was pessimistic for these outcomes.
2. **Wearable outcomes are daytime-weighted, not overnight.** The filtered data has few overnight segments, so an overnight measure is not supportable. Steps were dropped because filtered segments undercount them. A few 2018 timestamps (clock errors outside the 2021 study) were dropped.
3. **3D lab, first playable version.** Procedural station module (racks, lockers, cargo bags, vents, laptops, cables, LED corner strips, hatch, window), four stations, camera glides between them, HUD with a wrist checklist and a control deck, a plain 2D "numbers" panel, pre-registration export. Rendering: three.js with AgX tone mapping, ambient occlusion (GTAO), bloom limited to light sources, shadows; quality drops automatically if the frame rate stays under 32 fps.
4. **Problems found by looking at full-resolution renders, and fixed.** The cabin was washed out (bloom caught the white walls); the shell's end caps sealed the window; the evidence chamber looked empty because the glass wrote depth over the motes, then blew out to white because 1,600 additive motes stacked; tokens never finished sticking (the spring pulled to a point 3 cm off the board while the stick check measured to the board); token letters were rotated a quarter turn; the "B" in the marker font read as "8" at token size, so token faces use Archivo.
5. **Opening on a daylight pass.** The CelesTrak epoch falls on Earth's night side; the app starts the clock at the first time after the epoch when the station is sunlit with the Sun well above its horizon (same real orbit, later moment).
6. **Verdict read once, at the end of the mission.** The false-call rate was measured for a single read at the planned end, so the app shows probabilities every day but gives the verdict only on the last day. Peeking-and-stopping would raise the false-call rate; it is not offered.
7. **Testing while the browser pane is hidden.** Browsers pause animation frames in hidden tabs, so the app exposes a dev-only step function (`__solAdvance`) and a `?auto=<scenario>` driver; `lab/scripts/shoot.sh` takes full-resolution screenshots with headless Edge and a throwaway profile.
8. **Validation studies run, pre-registered first** (`docs/STUDIES_PREREG.md`, `docs/STUDIES_RESULTS.md`). Two bars failed and are reported as failures: plug-in crew pooling undercovered (fixed by integrating over between-person spread, Study 2b, pre-registered after the failure), and the circadian model locks to a Mars sol more easily than the pre-registered reading of the 2007 human study (the clock station now carries that caveat).
9. **Prior-art correction.** The Mars-sol light-timing result is published (2007); the app presents its own simulation as a replication, not a discovery.
10. **Crew mode added.** Four crew members, own randomized schedules, own verdicts, sharpened by validated pooling.
11. **A syntax error shipped once** (commit b219aca; a mismatched quote broke the whole app). Found because headless screenshots came back blank. `npm run check` now parses every file before commits.
12. **Simulated sleep data is now real people's data.** Gaussian noise produced impossible values (negative minutes to fall asleep). Each simulated person is now one of the 49 diary participants: their own typical level, with days resampled from their own logged deviations, kept within physical limits. Wearable measures keep the Gaussian model (values far from any limit).
13. **Redesigned as a professional crew health console** (feedback: "make everything look more like a professional tool", "hella buggy and glitchy"). Dark console layout: tabs along the top, an inspector on the right, a mission timeline at the bottom that drives every station (play, speed, crew selection). The floating HUD and wrist checklist were removed.
14. **Glitches traced and removed.** The clock deck was rebuilt every half second (clicks lost, open sections snapped shut); worker results re-rendered the whole deck (focus lost mid-typing); the camera drifted with the pointer, bobbed and rolled; floating objects crossed station views; a tab switch after the first one did nothing (a stale element reference). Panels now mount once and update values in place; the camera holds still between moves; laptops, floaters and cargo bags were cleared from in front of every station.
15. **Three new health stations, driven by one crew health model** (`lab/src/sim/health.js`, 5 tests). 14 pre-flight days plus 165 mission days for four crew. Medical: sleep from real diary participants; heart rate and HRV from published spreads; resting core temperature drifts up about 1 °C over 2.5 months (Stahn et al. 2017), shown as expected rather than flagged. Each measure is compared with that person's own pre-flight range (t prediction interval, clipped to physical limits). Air: CO2 with occasional reduced-scrubber days, the 3 mmHg nominal limit, and headache risk from the 7-day average (Law et al. 2014); temperature and humidity within NASA-STD-3001. Galley: a 14-item daily menu built from 19 USDA FoodData Central foods (pinned ids, local SR Legacy release), totals against NASA JSC-67378 targets. In-flight energy intake runs below targets, as reported for real crews.
16. **What is simulated is labelled where it is shown.** Every inspector panel ends with its sources and which values are simulated.
17. **Real space-agency and space-company data added** (request: "use actual NASA and other space company test data"). Radiation: 123,695 DOSTEL-1 readings from NASA OSDR RadLab. The derived median of 304 µGy a day matches DLR's published 250 to 290 µGy a day for this detector, and the hottest map cell sits over South America (the South Atlantic Anomaly), which is where it should be; 57% of the dose came from about 53 minutes a day there. Bloodwork: the four Inspiration4 (SpaceX) astronauts' clinical panels from NASA OSDR. A test file checks both datasets for physical plausibility.
18. **Data left out on purpose.** The OSD-575 multiplex cardiovascular panel was downloaded but not used: haptoglobin varied by a factor of about 500 within one person and C-reactive protein read far above clinical norms, so it cannot be compared with clinical ranges. Hemoglobin is labelled "percent" in the OSD-569 file, but its values and range are g/dL; the app uses g/dL. The NASA 600 mSv career limit is in effective dose and the detector measures absorbed dose in silicon, so the app does not put the two on one scale.
19. **Tool features.** Keyboard shortcuts (1 to 9 stations, Space play, arrows step a day), a downloadable crew report (vitals, air, radiation, 14-day nutrition, bloodwork flags, sources), and a radiation station whose globe marker flies a real day of ISS track coloured by the measured dose rate.
20. **Cabin air is now real.** 358 days of ISS telemetry from NASA OSDR's Environmental Data App replace the simulated cabin. 42 flat-lined sensor days (one value all day, e.g. 0.152 mmHg) and 31 duplicate days from overlapping missions were removed and are counted on screen. RR-7 and RR-9 disagree by about 3 mmHg in late August 2018; the row with more readings is kept, and this is listed as an open problem. The replayed days show the station above today's 3 mmHg limit in 2016, which matches the history of ISS CO2.
21. **Radiation by module.** REM detectors logged daily averages in six US-segment modules over the same days of Dec 2019: 324 (US Lab) to 648 µGy a day (Node 1), a factor of two inside one station.
22. **Gap analysis written** (`docs/GAP_ANALYSIS.md`): scorecard against VITA, the strongest public rival, and 100 problems ranked by severity.
23. **Built to the official challenge text.** The brief asks software to gather indicators and help astronauts act. New Check-in tab: the astronaut logs blood pressure, resting heart rate, fasting blood sugar, temperature, sleep, mood (1 to 5) and exercise minutes. Each indicator has how-to instructions for space, why spaceflight changes it, and when it matters. Each value gets one of three levels (within range / keep an eye on it / talk to the flight surgeon) with actions. Cut-offs: ACC/AHA 2017 (blood pressure), ADA (blood sugar), CDC fever definition, AHA heart rate range, AASM sleep need; escalation points without a published source (resting heart rate above 120 or below 40, three short nights, three low-mood days, three days under 30 minutes of exercise) are marked as team choices needing clinician review. Mood and exercise cover the two NASA hazards we were missing (isolation, altered gravity). The overview lists the five HRP hazards linked to the stations that track them. Entries are kept in the browser; unlogged days are filled from the model or simulated and labelled. 7 new tests.
24. **A patch landed in the wrong place** (a report section went into the Radiation panel, which then failed to open). Found by switching through every tab in the browser. Since then the headless sweep visits every tab and prints any page error.
25. **Clickable body figure.** A holographic 3D crew member with eight regions (brain and mood, eyes, heart and vessels, lungs, gut and metabolism, bones, muscles, blood and immune). Clicking a region, in 3D or in the list, shows what it does, how spaceflight affects it, which hazards apply, and today's linked measurements with their levels. Colour is the most serious level among them; eyes say "not measured" instead of guessing. Built from simple shapes and picked by mesh name, so a teammate's detailed model can replace it.
26. **Daily routine and reminders.** Seven measurements have times on the mission clock (06:30 heart rate to 21:00 mood). The timeline shows what is due; the check-in lists the routine with logged, due and later. At 1 h/s a quiet card appears when an item comes due (no sound, fades after 9 s). The mission clock now starts at 07:12 and arrow keys keep the time of day.
27. **Maps.** Orbit and radiation: equirectangular world map with the SGP4 ground track (45 minutes back, three orbits ahead), the night side from the Sun's position, the subsolar point, and the measured dose-rate grid; the info line gives the measured dose rate for the patch under the station. Station modules: simplified ISS layout coloured by measured REM dose. Life support: CO2 removal, CO2 reduction, water recovery and oxygen generation as a flow diagram with the replayed day's real cabin values (flows illustrative).
28. **CSS bug:** classes that set `display` overrode the `hidden` attribute, so the map overlay and reminder card showed on every tab. A global `[hidden] { display: none !important }` fixes it.
29. **Licence.** Apache 2.0 at the repo root, the Space Apps default for open-source entries.
30. **Deployable build and CI.** Asset paths are relative (`base: "./"`), so the build runs from any folder; verified by serving `lab/dist` from a subfolder: all data, textures and the power worker load, all tabs open. A GitHub Actions workflow runs `check`, `test` and `build` on every push and deploys `main` to GitHub Pages once the repo is public and Pages is set to "GitHub Actions".
31. **Drop-in models.** `lab/public/models/models.json` lists `.glb`/`.gltf` files that replace a station's props (live-data parts such as screens, the radiation globe and airflow stay) or the body figure; meshes named after a body part (heart, brain, ...) become clickable and take the status colours. Tested with a small generated glTF whose "Heart" mesh became the clickable heart; hidden built-in parts are excluded from clicks. Guide: `docs/MODELS.md`.
32. **Guided tour and mission patches.** A 10-step tour (opens on first visit, `?tour=1`, or the Tour button) walks through the story: own normal, check-in, body, trends, real bloodwork, real air, real radiation, maps, experiments. Eleven mission patches reward habits (first entry, full check-in, 3- and 7-day streaks, every body part, every station, every map, a finished experiment, crew mode, a report, the tour); nothing is ever lost and no day is punished. Tab labels were shortened so all twelve fit.
33. **Teammate's work merged** (ktm331, PR #1): procedural surface detail on the module, station navigation (hotspot dots, edge arrows, Left/Right between stations, Escape to overview), overview camera parked outside the hatch with a clip plane, steadier bloom and LED strips, calmer body clock. Conflicts were in `showTab` only; both sides kept (their per-view navigation, our first-view cut and panel tidying). Day stepping moved to Shift+Left/Right as they set it.
34. **Less text on screen.** Each panel's introduction and source notes now sit in a closed "About this view" section at the bottom; KPI notes were cut to a few words; vitals lines read "Usual 66 to 97 bpm" instead of a sentence; the overview folds the patches and the five hazards. Project-level information (data, method, notices) moved to an About window.
35. **Sharper text in 3D.** Hand-written tape labels (marker font) were replaced by printed labels drawn at twice the resolution with bold Archivo; station screens are drawn at 1.5 to 2 times resolution with fewer, larger numbers and no footnotes; every texture uses the GPU's maximum anisotropic filtering. The medical screen had been redrawing and re-uploading every frame because it compared a fresh object each time; it now redraws 12 times a second or when the day changes.
36. **Instruments tab.** Seven devices: blood pressure unit (CDL Halter unit on the ISS), pulse oximeter, glucose meter (reference class), smart garment (CSA Bio-Monitor and Astroskin), DOSTEL dosimeter, CDRA CO2 removal, T2 treadmill. Each is drawn as a blueprint (translucent solids, white outlines, grid) that explodes apart; labels are HTML so the text is crisp; click a part or its row to highlight it. Parts are teaching diagrams of how each class of device works, not flight drawings, and say so. Documentation: `docs/INSTRUMENTS.md`, generated from the same data.
37. **Universal import.** Any device that exports CSV or a FHIR R4 bundle can feed the check-in: Observations are matched by LOINC code (8480-6, 8462-4, 8867-4, 2339-0, 1558-6, 8310-5, 93832-4), units converted (mmol/L, °F, minutes), out-of-range values skipped and counted, and imported values labelled "imported". Tested in unit tests and end to end in the browser (a FHIR file's blood pressure and glucose appeared on the check-in cards).
38. **A deleted export slipped through `npm run check`** (it only parses files). The page sweep caught it. New `npm run verify` runs check, tests and a production build, which fails on missing exports.
39. **Judge's-view pass** (`docs/JUDGE_REVIEW.md`, `lab/scripts/judge_walk.mjs`). A fresh browser profile at 1366 by 768 followed the tour. Found and fixed: the camera position became NaN when the page loaded at zero size (every later move then failed); GSAP lag smoothing stretched camera moves to about a minute on very slow frames; first visit opened on an empty day 0; the key hint sat under the tour card; no one-line statement on the overview; README and pitch out of date and not in the brief's words; no Earth-benefit statement; blood values lost their measured precision. Estimated mean across the 11 criteria went from 7.9 to 8.5; the remaining blockers are a live link, a demo video, the teammate's 3D models and a clinician review.
40. **Lag fixed with measurements.** `lab/scripts/perf.mjs` (headless Edge on the real GPU) plus a per-frame CPU meter in the app (`window.__solCpu`). Overview CPU per frame about 41 ms to about 9 ms: static module merge (`scene/bake.js mergeStatic`, 625 meshes into 68), label atlas (`atlasLabels`, about 80 draws into 2, about 80 MB of textures freed), shadows drawn once, ambient occlusion opt-in (`?q=ultra`), lighter duct ribs, vitals pulse as a scrolling texture, panel refresh only on change, one-time resolution drop on slow GPUs, loading screen removed after its fade even in throttled tabs. While profiling, throwaway browser profiles filled the disk (about 5 GB); the scripts now use a minimal cache and delete their profile.
41. **Five sections instead of thirteen tabs**, compact check-in rows, folded routine, still hotspot dots, three speeds; **hologram shader** for the body; **4-step quick start** with spotlights for first visits.
