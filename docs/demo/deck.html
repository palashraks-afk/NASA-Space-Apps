02 / 07

The problem

Far from Earth, astronauts have to judge their own health.

  • From Mars, a message to Earth takes up to 22 minutes each way. No doctor on board.
  • Population "normal" ranges miss changes that are big for one person but ordinary for another.
  • Constant alarms wear crews out, and a lucky week can look like a cure.

NASA's five hazards of human spaceflight

04 / 07

Inside the station

A 3D module with a body you can click.

  • Each organ lights up by status. Click it for today's readings and what spaceflight does to it.
  • Real body proportions, in the floating posture NASA measured in astronauts on the ISS.
  • Stations for vitals, bloodwork, air, radiation, food, body clock and experiments.
05 / 07

Built on NASA data

  • 123,695 real ISS radiation readings (NASA OSDR RadLab, DLR DOSTEL). Over half the dose comes over the South Atlantic.
  • 358 days of ISS cabin CO2, temperature and humidity (NASA OSDR Environmental Data App).
  • Inspiration4 crew blood tests (NASA OSDR OSD-569, OSD-575).
  • Sleep diaries of 49 people; USDA food data; ISS orbit from CelesTrak.

Act, and prove it works

When something drifts, the crew runs a fair self-experiment.

Pick a change, lock the plan, let the schedule randomize the days. The answer can be "works", "backfires", "no difference" or "not settled yet".
