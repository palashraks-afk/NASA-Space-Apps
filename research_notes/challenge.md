**Create Health Monitoring Software for Astronauts on Space Missions.** Difficulty: Intermediate. Subjects: Human Exploration, Software, Space Exploration.

## Summary

Long-duration missions expose astronauts to space radiation, isolation and confinement, altered gravity, and a hostile closed environment, which can bring immune changes, bone loss, cardiovascular events, and behavioral health problems. On long missions, astronauts carry much of the responsibility for spotting these changes in themselves. Your challenge is to build a health monitoring software that gathers health indicators and enables astronauts to evaluate and act on the status of their health.

## Background (key points)

- NASA's Human Research Program names five hazards: space radiation, isolation and confinement, distance from Earth, gravity fields, hostile closed environments.
- They contribute to immune changes, bone density loss, cardiovascular events, inadequate nutrition, behavioral health conditions and others.
- Astronauts must be able to assess themselves during a mission, to prevent and treat problems.
- Example indicators: blood pressure, heart rate, blood sugar, body temperature, daily sleep hours, mood.

## Objectives (key points)

- A **visual tool** that helps astronauts **assess their own health** and **provides guidance on treatments and health improvements**.
- Data may be gathered from the user through **any input**: keyboard text, mouse clicks, camera images. Any data that represents a health indicator (blood sugar in mg/dL, ultrasound images).
- Use NASA HRP resources and public resources from other agencies to choose indicators and support a quick assessment.
- Questions to consider: which measurements can be performed in space; why is an exam needed; how hazards affect each indicator; **when a result warrants attention from the crew or flight surgeons**; **what actions prevent a critical condition**.
- UI matters; animations and interactivity encouraged. Example: **a human figure where each body part is clickable** and shows its function and health status.

## Potential considerations (optional)

- Different environments: spacecraft, space station, rover, base on another planet or moon.
- **Prompt astronauts to take measurements or complete actions** as part of the mission routine.
- Features: **gamification**, **dynamic charts over time**, **color coding** for status at a glance.
- **Do not assume astronauts know how to assess their health**: simple instructions and explanations of measurements, indicators and risks.
