# NASA Moon Base Challenge — overview and fit check

Source: https://moonbasechallenge.org/ (pasted by the team 2026-11-17) + https://moonbasechallenge.org/rules/ (fetched 2026-11-17). This is a **separate competition from NASA Space Apps** (which Sol Lab was built for) — different administrator, different format, different eligibility rules. Documented here for the record and to flag a hard eligibility gate before anyone invests time in it.

## ⚠️ Eligibility gate — check this first

This is a **currently-enrolled-student-only** competition, administered via NASA STEM Gateway + the Institute of Competition Sciences (ICS) platform, not an open hackathon:

- **Team:** 2–5 student members + 1 adult Team Coach (not a member, 18+, U.S. person, formally affiliated with the team's organization).
- **Student divisions** (pick one per team):
  - **High School:** ages 13–18 at registration, enrolled grade 9+, not yet graduated, U.S. citizen/LPR/eligible foreign national.
  - **Two-Year:** age 16+, enrolled undergrad at an accredited U.S. two-year institution.
  - **Four-Year:** age 16+, enrolled undergrad at an accredited U.S. four-year institution.
- **Ineligible:** middle school or below, graduate students, non-U.S.-institution students, ICS employees/affiliates/family.
- **Work must be the students' own** — the Coach may only give logistical help; all design, analysis, visuals, and writing must be student-authored. AI use is allowed but must be disclosed and described.
- Submissions are **anonymized** (no names, no institution, no NASA logos) when judged.

Nothing in this repo records the team members' ages, schools or grades. (An earlier version of this note called the team "adult-led"; that came from a recruiting blurb on the Space Apps team page and is not evidence of anything.) **Before treating this as a real target, confirm each member's school, grade and age against the rules above, and whether an adult at a U.S. school or club will be the Team Coach.** This is a hard eligibility screen (section 11.1) that disqualifies incomplete or ineligible entries outright. The plan for what to change is in `moon-base-fit-plan.md`.

## What it actually is

Not a working-software hackathon. It's a **two-phase paper + pitch competition**:

| Phase | Deliverable | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| **Project Phase** | Concept Overview Paper | 5–7 pages (excl. cover/references), 11pt+ font, PDF, English only, ≤6MB |
| **Final Phase** (finalists only) | Recorded Pitch Video + Slide Deck | Video ≤10 min, public/unlisted, every student on camera, no AI avatars/voices, no coach on camera; Slides ≤15, 16:9, PDF |

No code submission, no GitHub repo, no live demo — the "product" here is the written concept and the pitch, not Sol Lab's 3D app itself. Any carryover from Sol Lab would be as **reference material / inspiration for a paper's Solution Design section**, not a direct port.

## Six topics (pick one)

1. Power Generation & Storage
2. Habitat Construction
3. Life-Support Systems
4. Surface Mobility
5. Communication Networks
6. **Astronaut Safety** — closest to Sol Lab's actual domain (health monitoring, risk detection), but note the brief is about *lunar surface* hazards/safety systems, not in-mission astronaut health software — would need reframing, not reuse, of the concept.

## Evaluation criteria (what judges actually score)

**Project Phase — 25 points, 5 criteria × 0–5:**
1. Problem Background and NASA Mission Alignment — cites credible research, links to NASA's sustained-lunar-presence goal.
2. Solution Design and Operations — core function, autonomy/robotics/in-situ-resource reasoning (no constant Earth link assumed).
3. Solution Viability — mass/volume/power budget; survives −170°C to +120°C; two-week light/dark cycles; low resupply cadence.
4. Visual Aids and Technical Illustration — at least one page of schematics/diagrams/storyboards.
5. Communication — clarity of the written paper itself.

**Final Phase — 25 points, 5 criteria × 0–5:** Project Overview & NASA Need; Solution & Visual Demonstration; Real-World Implementation & Constraints; Systems Impact/Risk Mitigation/Design Iteration; Technical Skills & Communication.

Judging process: Challenge Administrator screens for completeness/eligibility first (disqualifies incomplete entries) → NASA SME/aerospace-professional panel scores with a standardized rubric → top entries go to a Judges' Summit vote for finalists/winners.

## Prizes (per the rules page — see caveats below)

- **Project Phase Finalists:** up to 100 teams, $1,400 each (~$140,000 total)
- **Final Phase Runners-Up:** up to 12, $10,000 each ($120,000)
- **Final Phase Grand Prize:** up to 12, $20,000 each ($240,000)
- Max total: ~$500,000, **contingent on federal funding and NASA approval — not guaranteed**. Paid to the Team Coach's U.S. bank account; Coach decides split; tax/banking/eligibility forms required first.
- Caveat: the rules page's own counts don't reconcile. It says "8 finalists per division per category" (3 divisions × 6 categories × 8 = 144) next to "up to 100", and "one per division per category" for 12 Grand Prizes (3 × 6 = 18). Treat the exact counts as unconfirmed and ask the organizers at Office Hours 1 (2027-01-14) if it matters.

## IP and process notes

- Teams keep IP; NASA may negotiate a non-exclusive license afterward.
- Plagiarism banned, sources must be cited, late submissions not accepted.
- Submitted through ICS's platform, not GitHub.

## Bottom line

This is a legitimate, well-funded program, but it's **not a drop-in second venue for Sol Lab** — different audience (enrolled students only), different deliverable (paper/pitch, not software), different platform (STEM Gateway/ICS, not GitHub/Space Apps), and a ~6-month timeline instead of a weekend. If the team doesn't meet the student/age eligibility rules above, this should be treated as informational only, not a target to build toward.
